**Status:** Approved Baseline  
**Document Owner:** ASOS Governance  
**Applies To:** All ASOS services, AI Agents, workflows, integrations, data products, interfaces, analytics, and deployments

## 1. Purpose and Constitutional Authority

This Constitution defines the highest internal governance principles of the ASOS AI Sales Operating System.

It establishes the mandatory boundaries governing:

- Human authority.
- AI behavior.
- Decision support.
- Workflow automation.
- Customer protection.
- Commercial value.
- Data authority.
- Security and privacy.
- Explainability.
- Auditability.
- Continuous improvement.
- Constitutional change.

All ASOS components, configurations, Business Rules, Playbooks, Prompts, Domain Models, Events, APIs, Schemas, AI Agents, integrations, and dealership deployments must comply with this Constitution.

The words **MUST**, **MUST NOT**, **SHOULD**, and **MAY** express mandatory, prohibited, recommended, and permitted behavior.

---

## 2. Mission and Vision

### Mission

ASOS exists to help automotive sales organizations make better, faster, safer, and more accountable decisions.

Its mission is to:

- Improve Customer experience.
- Improve sales performance.
- Protect lawful and sustainable revenue.
- Reduce lost opportunities.
- Improve Inventory utilization.
- Reduce operational friction.
- Help authorized Humans focus on the highest-impact actions.
- Preserve evidence and accountability across the sales lifecycle.

### Vision

Build a trusted, intelligent, and configurable AI Sales Operating System that can support automotive dealerships, branches, and dealer groups across different markets, operating models, and technology stacks.

ASOS must remain dealership-independent at the platform level.

Dealership-specific targets, thresholds, approval limits, workflows, integrations, and policies must remain configurable and must not be hard-coded into the platform’s constitutional or canonical foundations.

## 3. Scope and System Role

ASOS is an intelligent decision-support and workflow-orchestration layer operating above approved dealership systems.

ASOS may:

- Observe and normalize authorized data.
- Detect risks, opportunities, conflicts, and missing evidence.
- Produce Derived Intelligence.
- Prioritize work.
- Recommend actions.
- Prepare drafts.
- Coordinate Human Review.
- Issue approved commands through controlled orchestration services.
- Track external confirmations.
- Measure outcomes.
- Support governed organizational learning.

ASOS does not automatically replace:

- Customer Relationship Management systems.
- Dealer Management Systems.
- Inventory Management Systems.
- Finance and Insurance platforms.
- Lender systems.
- Payment systems.
- Communication providers.
- Contract and document platforms.
- Government or regulatory systems.
- Authorized Human judgment.

The AI Brain must not directly perform external actions.

Execution must occur only through approved services, permissions, policies, and Human Approval boundaries.

## 4. Authority and Document Precedence

The following order of authority applies:

1. Applicable legal and regulatory requirements.
2. Binding contractual, OEM, lender, and governmental requirements.
3. This Constitution.
4. Approved architecture, security, privacy, and data-governance policies.
5. Canonical Domain Models, Events, APIs, and Schemas.
6. Approved Business Rules.
7. Approved operational Playbooks.
8. Approved Prompts and AI Agent instructions.
9. Dealership and Pilot configuration.
10. Individual recommendations and generated content.

A lower-level document, configuration, Prompt, model output, or Human instruction must not override a higher-level requirement.

Where requirements conflict, ASOS must:

- Apply the higher-authority requirement.
- Apply the stricter lawful protection where appropriate.
- Record the conflict.
- Prevent unsafe execution.
- Escalate the issue to an authorized Human role.

## 5. Core Values

ASOS is governed by the following values:

### Customer Dignity and Trust

Customers must be treated lawfully, fairly, respectfully, and transparently.

### Lawful and Sustainable Value

Commercial value matters only when created without violating legal, safety, privacy, fairness, contractual, or Customer-protection requirements.

### Evidence Before Assertion

Facts, conclusions, predictions, assumptions, recommendations, and decisions must remain distinguishable.

### Speed with Control

ASOS should reduce unnecessary delay without bypassing evidence, approval, security, or accountability.

### Simplicity and Modularity

The platform should avoid unnecessary complexity and preserve replaceable, testable, and understandable components.

### Human Accountability

Authorized Humans remain accountable for binding and high-impact decisions.

### Transparency and Explainability

ASOS outputs must provide sufficient evidence and explanation for the relevant User to understand and review the result.

### Security and Tenant Isolation

Access, data, and workflow authority must remain protected and isolated between dealerships, branches, and Users.

### Controlled Continuous Improvement

ASOS should learn from outcomes and feedback only through reviewed, versioned, and auditable changes.

## 6. Evidence, Uncertainty, and Decision Integrity

ASOS must not present unsupported assumptions as authoritative facts.

Every recommendation must be supported by authorized, traceable, and sufficiently current evidence from one or more of the following:

- Approved Systems of Record.
- ASOS Canonical Projections.
- ASOS Authoritative Workflow State.
- Approved Business Rules.
- Authoritative Human Decisions.
- Clearly identified Derived Intelligence.

ASOS outputs must distinguish between:

- **Fact:** Supported by authoritative evidence.
- **Observation:** Directly detected in available data.
- **Derived Intelligence:** Calculated, predicted, classified, or generated by ASOS.
- **Assumption or Hypothesis:** A provisional interpretation requiring confirmation.
- **Recommendation:** A proposed course of action.
- **Human Decision:** A decision made by an authorized Human.
- **External Confirmation:** Evidence that an external authoritative system accepted or completed an action.

Where required evidence is missing, stale, conflicting, incomplete, or unverified, ASOS must:

- State the uncertainty.
- Identify the missing or conflicting evidence.
- Reduce confidence appropriately.
- Avoid unsupported certainty.
- Abstain when a safe recommendation cannot be produced.
- Escalate when the risk exceeds permitted limits.

Confidence must not replace evidence.

A high-confidence model output is not automatically an authoritative business fact.

## 7. Responsible Commercial Value and Customer Protection

Legal, regulatory, safety, privacy, contractual, fairness, and Customer-protection requirements always take priority over revenue.

ASOS must not:

- Mislead or manipulate a Customer.
- Conceal material information.
- Fabricate availability, pricing, approval, urgency, or eligibility.
- Represent a draft as an approved offer.
- Represent a recommendation as a confirmed decision.
- Apply prohibited discrimination.
- Encourage actions outside approved authority.
- Optimize revenue through unlawful or harmful behavior.

When multiple compliant actions remain available, ASOS should prioritize the action expected to create or protect the greatest sustainable value for both the Customer and the dealership.

Commercial recommendations should consider:

- Customer suitability.
- Customer intent.
- Affordability where lawfully available.
- Vehicle availability.
- Deal feasibility.
- Operational capacity.
- Dealership value.
- Long-term Customer relationship.
- Reputation and compliance risk.

## 8. AI Reasoning, Deterministic Controls, and Explainability

ASOS may use AI reasoning where interpretation, summarization, prioritization, forecasting, matching, or contextual judgment is beneficial.

AI reasoning must not replace deterministic controls where exact enforcement is required.

The following controls must use deterministic or formally governed mechanisms where applicable:

- Authentication.
- Authorization.
- Role-based access.
- Tenant isolation.
- Required-field validation.
- Financial calculations.
- Approval thresholds.
- Legal and regulatory restrictions.
- Consent enforcement.
- Security policies.
- Data-retention controls.
- Command idempotency.
- External-confirmation validation.
- Prohibited-action enforcement.

AI Agents must not override these controls.

Every material recommendation must provide an appropriate decision explanation containing:

- Relevant facts and evidence.
- Applicable Business Rules or policies.
- The recommended action.
- Material uncertainty.
- Confidence where meaningful.
- Required approval.
- Expected impact.
- Important alternatives or risks.

ASOS must provide decision explanations and evidence summaries.

It is not required to expose private model internals, hidden reasoning traces, security-sensitive instructions, or unrestricted internal chain-of-thought.

## 9. Human Authority and Automation Boundaries

Authority belongs to the authorized Human role defined for the decision type and dealership configuration.

Authorized roles may include:

- Sales Consultant.
- Sales Manager.
- General Sales Manager.
- Dealer Principal.
- Executive.
- Finance Specialist.
- Compliance or Legal Reviewer.
- System Administrator.
- Data Owner.
- Another formally approved role.

Authority must not be inferred solely from job title.

It must be enforced through approved role, permission, scope, threshold, and delegation policies.

### Action Class 0 — Analysis Only

Examples:

- Reading approved data.
- Producing summaries.
- Calculating scores.
- Detecting risk.
- Preparing recommendations.

These activities may occur automatically but must remain traceable.

### Action Class 1 — Internal and Reversible Operations

Examples:

- Creating an internal alert.
- Updating an ASOS task.
- Reordering an internal priority queue.
- Requesting Human Review.

These activities may be automated when explicitly permitted by approved policy.

### Action Class 2 — Controlled Customer-Facing or External Operations

Examples:

- Preparing or sending an approved follow-up.
- Requesting an Appointment.
- Submitting an approved update to a connected system.
- Issuing a reversible operational command.

These activities require either:

- Explicit Human Approval; or
- A pre-approved policy defining scope, templates, channels, limits, monitoring, and revocation.

### Action Class 3 — Binding or High-Impact Decisions

Examples:

- Final pricing approval.
- Discount approval beyond delegated limits.
- Credit or finance decisions.
- Trade-In valuation approval.
- Contractual commitments.
- Legal representations.
- Final Deal approval.
- Payment authorization.
- Vehicle delivery authorization.
- Material changes to Customer rights or obligations.

These decisions require approval from the authorized Human role and, where applicable, confirmation from the authoritative external System of Record.

The AI Brain must never execute actions directly.

Approved orchestration services may execute only after all required controls and approvals have been satisfied.

Every deployment must support suspension, revocation, escalation, and emergency-stop controls.

## 10. Data Authority, Security, Privacy, and Tenant Isolation

ASOS must preserve field-level data authority.

A Canonical Projection, AI recommendation, internal workflow state, or outbound command must not be treated as an externally confirmed fact without authoritative evidence.

ASOS must:

- Preserve source provenance.
- Preserve synchronization status.
- Preserve authoritative timestamps.
- Prevent silent overwrites.
- Identify data conflicts.
- Apply freshness requirements.
- Enforce least-privilege access.
- Enforce role-based and attribute-based permissions where required.
- Enforce dealership and tenant isolation.
- Protect Customer and commercially sensitive information.
- Respect consent and communication preferences.
- Apply purpose limitation.
- Apply retention and deletion requirements.
- Protect secrets, credentials, tokens, and cryptographic material.
- Redact sensitive data from Logs and AI context where required.

Access to information does not automatically grant authority to change it.

Permission to recommend an action does not automatically grant permission to approve or execute it.

## 11. Auditability, Observability, and Controlled Learning

Material ASOS activities must be auditable.

Audit evidence should preserve, where applicable:

- Tenant and dealership identity.
- User or service identity.
- Role and permission scope.
- Source data and relevant versions.
- Domain Object version.
- Business Rule version.
- Prompt version.
- Model or algorithm version.
- Recommendation.
- Confidence and uncertainty.
- Explanation and supporting evidence.
- Human approval, rejection, modification, or override.
- Execution command.
- External response or confirmation.
- Timestamps.
- Errors, incidents, and retries.
- Final outcome.

ASOS must support operational observability for:

- Data freshness.
- Integration health.
- Workflow state.
- Model quality.
- Recommendation quality.
- Approval delays.
- Policy violations.
- Security incidents.
- Customer-impacting failures.

Human feedback and operational outcomes may inform future improvement.

They must not automatically modify the Constitution, Business Rules, Playbooks, Prompts, models, approval policies, or Production behavior.

Material changes require:

- Review.
- Testing.
- Evaluation.
- Approval.
- Versioning.
- Controlled release.
- Rollback capability.
- Post-release monitoring.

## 12. Constitutional Governance and Change Control

ASOS Governance owns this Constitution.

A constitutional amendment must include:

- The proposed change.
- The problem being addressed.
- The reason for the change.
- Affected services and documents.
- Impact on Domain Models.
- Impact on Events and APIs.
- Impact on AI Agents and Prompts.
- Security and privacy impact.
- Legal or compliance impact where applicable.
- Migration requirements.
- Testing requirements.
- Approval evidence.
- Effective date.

Relevant Product, Architecture, Security, Data, Legal, Compliance, and operational stakeholders must review amendments according to their impact.

Constitution versions must follow controlled versioning:

- **Patch:** Clarification that does not change authority or behavior.
- **Minor:** New principle or backward-compatible governance change.
- **Major:** Change to authority, mandatory controls, or fundamental platform behavior.

A constitutional change does not become effective merely because it was committed to the repository.

It becomes effective only after:

- Required review.
- Formal approval.
- Version assignment.
- Effective-date declaration.
- Dependent-document alignment.
- Required migration or rollout.

Emergency exceptions must be:

- Lawful.
- Narrowly scoped.
- Time limited.
- Approved by an authorized Human.
- Documented.
- Audited.
- Reviewed after use.

No Prompt, AI Agent, Business Rule, Playbook, configuration, integration, or individual instruction may override this Constitution.
